Optimization

Four rounds, one byte-identical teapot

2026-11-18 · Onyx Infer engineering notes

The rule

No performance conclusion without evidence. Every optimization candidate must bring three things: a committed measurement JSON, passing correctness gates, and — for the native pipeline — the output hash of a fixed reference request (the teapot, seed 42). If a candidate is accepted, the diff proves the pixels did not move.

Round 1 made things slower

Theory said caching grouped zero-points should help; measurement said 42.63 s against a 42.30 s baseline. The v1 review publishes that row anyway. We would rather have a small truthful number than a big story — an optimization log you cannot trust is worse than no log.

Why byte-identical matters

  • It converts a subjective quality debate into one hex string:

d3e3228138e309c627d0c48e2bcbb1597a18fdf5c2ebccc1767c1ecef232b8c8.

  • It lets us chase aggressive tricks — round 4 fuses five operations into one dispatch and

replaces copies with MPSNDArray offset views — at full speed, because if the hash moves, the change moves out.

  • It makes automated optimization safe. Our Loop controller can only submit candidates

through the public protocols and must attach evidence; it cannot redefine what "correct" means.

Where this goes

The next rounds aim at the two gaps we publish rather than hide: our own Q4 GEMM kernel is still ~16× slower than MLX's quantized_matmul on the same shape, and the community MLX W8A8 path is ~1.64× faster end-to-end on the same request. Those numbers live in benchmarks/results/, right next to the code that produced them.
