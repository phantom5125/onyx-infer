Memory

Sixteen megapixels on 36 GB of unified memory

2026-11-18 · Onyx Infer engineering notes

The budget is smaller than the weights

The target machine grants 28.08 GiB of recommended GPU working set and caps a single buffer at 21.06 GiB. Against that: a 21.86 GB W4 text pack and a 37.47 GB W4 DiT pack (high-precision AdaLN projections included). Neither pair fits resident at once; the DiT does not even fit by itself. "Unified memory" does not mean "no memory management" — it means the management is visible, plannable, and ours.

Phase residency, not one resident graph

The engine executes in phases with explicit handoffs:

  1. Text phase — the Qwen3-VL text tower streams block-by-block, keeps the layer-50 hidden

states, then releases the pack.

  1. DiT phase — the 37.47 GB pack streams block-by-block. Timestep and AdaLN projections

are precomputed once, their weights released; each transformer block's weights are evicted after use.

  1. VAE phase — the FP32 2D-ViT decoder (2.34 GB) runs with tiling on the 16×-downsampled

latent grid.

Attention must be chunked

At 4096×4096, H3's design — one image token per 32×32 pixel patch — yields (4096/32)² = 16,384 image tokens, before text and reference tokens lengthen the sequence. A materialized attention matrix is out of the question at 16 MP; SDPA runs on chunked windows. The tile experiments behind this live in benchmarks/results/m5-max-36gb-q4-tile32-v1.json.

The result

The 4096×4096 teapot request: 329.61 s, peak 2,144,190,464 bytes (~2.14 GB), full RunRecord stored and replayed. This is direct generation — the DiT saw a native 4K token grid — not a diffusion pass at 1024 followed by an upscaler, which is why structure and label text survive at 16 MP.

What we will not claim

W4 output at these resolutions passes our functional and visual checks on reference fixtures. That is not a broad quality-equivalence claim: multi-prompt evaluation, more resolutions and more seeds are queued in the Loop. Published numbers first; adjectives later.
